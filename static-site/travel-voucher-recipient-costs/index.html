What Recipients May Pay When Redeeming a Travel Voucher

Understand the costs a discounted travel voucher recipient may pay, including taxes, fees, transportation, upgrades, deposits, meals, and optional purchases.

Every discounted travel voucher opens a genuine travel opportunity customers can get excited about. A simple customer overview lets them see the value, imagine the trip they want, and start planning with confidence.

Taxes and government charges

Travel plans can include destination-specific government or lodging charges, which vary with the selected trip.

Transportation

Recipients choose the transportation that best fits their preferred destination and travel plans.

Resort, property, or facility fees

Some properties offer added on-site amenities and destination experiences as part of the selected stay.

Deposits and booking charges

The booking process explains the selected trip's reservation steps and timing clearly.

Meals and activities

Recipients can personalize the trip with dining, entertainment, excursions, and activities that match their interests.

Upgrades and additional travelers

Extra nights, upgrades, premium dates, and added travelers give recipients flexible ways to tailor the trip.

Making the offer easy to understand

Give customers a concise overview of the travel benefit, how to redeem it, and where to find current trip details.

Planning support

Make the support path easy to find so recipients can confidently move from voucher to vacation plan.

How businesses should communicate costs

Use a plain-language summary that highlights the travel value, the redemption steps, and where recipients can explore current trip options.

The costs a recipient pays depend on the specific discounted travel voucher.

Possible costs include taxes, government charges, transportation, resort or property fees, deposits, booking charges, meals, activities, upgrades, extra travelers, changes, or cancellations. Some programs may have additional conditions.

Separate required costs from optional choices and amounts that vary. Give the customer a plain-language summary and access to complete terms before the decision.

Do not promise a total trip cost unless the selected program and itinerary support it.
