How to Handle “I Want to Shop Around”

Respond when a prospect wants to shop around by defining comparison criteria, uncovering uncertainty, and setting a useful next decision.

Schedule a Campaign Consultation

Shopping around is a natural part of a high-ticket purchase. A confident response helps the buyer compare clearly while keeping your value at the center of the decision.

Acknowledge the buyer's process

“That makes sense. This is an important purchase.”

Then ask what the buyer hopes to learn from another option:

“Which parts of the decision do you most want to compare?”

The answer may be price, product fit, service, financing, warranty, implementation, or confidence.

Build comparison criteria together

Create a short checklist based on the customer's priorities. Include total price, scope, service, support, timeline, operating cost, protection, and risk where relevant.

Create a comparison checklist that makes the full value of the purchase easy to see. That clarity builds credibility and lets your strongest advantages stand out.

Test whether another concern is hiding

Ask:

“If another option did not improve the areas we listed, would you feel comfortable moving forward with this one?”

If the buyer still hesitates, shopping is not the only concern. Continue diagnosis.

Agree on the next decision

Set a clear follow-up: ask when the comparisons will be complete, who else will participate, and what decision should happen next.

“You are meeting the other provider Tuesday. Shall we compare the two written proposals Wednesday at three?”

Use an incentive to add a reason to choose

A discounted travel voucher can make a qualifying purchase stand apart from look-alike alternatives. Bring the travel experience to life and include that added value in the customer's complete comparison.

If the offer has a real deadline, state it clearly and position the added value as a timely advantage.
