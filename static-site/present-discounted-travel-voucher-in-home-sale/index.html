How to Present a Discounted Travel Voucher During an In-Home Sale

Present a discounted travel voucher during an in-home sale after project fit, contractor trust, value, affordability, authority, and eligibility are confirmed.

Schedule a Campaign Consultation

The home is a high-trust environment. The voucher should be presented calmly, accurately, and only after the homeowner understands the project. It must add value to a sound decision rather than increase pressure.

Confirm the project without the promotion

Ask whether the scope addresses the homeowner's goals and whether the contractor, schedule, warranty, implementation, and investment make sense. Involve the required decision-makers.

If the answer is no, continue working on the project or stop.

Diagnose the remaining concern

Determine whether the hesitation is scope, proof, contractor trust, affordability, price-value, authority, timing, disruption, or another comparison. The voucher is most relevant when timing or desire for added value remains.

Confirm eligibility

Verify the qualifying project, transaction threshold, campaign dates, combination rules, and manager approval before making the offer.

Ask permission to explain it

“For qualifying projects during this campaign, we include a discounted travel voucher. May I show you exactly what it provides, what the recipient pays, and the applicable dates?”

Explain the complete recipient terms

Cover included travel value, recipient-paid costs, amounts that vary, availability, restrictions, activation, booking or redemption, deadlines, changes, cancellation, and support. Provide the written terms.

Check understanding

Ask what questions the homeowners have about inclusions, costs, dates, or process. Do not rely only on “Does that make sense?”

Return to the project decision

“Does that added value affect your timing?”

If yes, ask whether they would like to move forward. If no, return to the actual concern. Do not stack additional pressure or concessions.

Record and follow through

Capture eligibility, presentation, issuance, objection, discount, decision, project value, gross profit, and follow-up. Ensure the recipient receives support information.
