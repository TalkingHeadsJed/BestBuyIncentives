Incentives for marketing agencies

Agencies can add a customer-facing incentive to client campaigns as a conversion lever that does not rely on discounting the client's product. It differentiates your proposals and gives you a measurable lift to report alongside media and creative.

By Karl Kramer, CEO of Best Buy Incentives
Published August 26, 2026 · Updated September 20, 2026
Schedule a Campaign Consultation

Why discounting is a weak lever for your clients

When a campaign underperforms, the reflex is to recommend a discount or a bigger media spend. Both are blunt instruments. A discount trains the client's customers to wait for the next sale and erodes the margin the client hired you to grow. More spend raises acquisition cost without changing why prospects hesitate. Neither gives you a differentiated story to tell in the next pitch.

A customer-facing incentive is a different kind of lever. It raises perceived value at the moment of decision, so the client's offer converts better without cutting price, and it gives your agency a conversion tool competitors are not bringing to the table.

How agencies add incentives to client campaigns

  • Offer enhancement. Layer a travel reward onto a client promotion so the call to action carries more pull without a price cut.
  • Lead-gen incentive. Use a reward to lift form fills, demo bookings, or showroom visits in the campaign funnel.
  • Proposal differentiator. Include the incentive strategy in pitches to win high-ticket clients who are tired of discount-driven results.
  • Retention and referral. Deploy rewards in loyalty and referral flows to extend campaign value past the first sale.

Measuring incentive lift alongside campaign KPIs

Because the incentive attaches to a defined action, its impact is measurable next to your existing KPIs. You can track conversion rate on incentivized versus control audiences, cost per acquisition with the reward factored in, and average order value on incentivized purchases. That gives you a clean before-and-after to report, and a repeatable playbook you can bring to the next client.

How it works for your agency, step by step

  1. Identify the conversion gap. Pinpoint where the client's funnel stalls.
  2. Design the incentive offer. Attach a travel reward to the action that needs a lift.
  3. Launch and measure. Run incentivized versus control and track conversion, CPA, and order value.
  4. Report and scale. Show the lift, then extend the incentive to other campaigns and clients.
  5. Redemption handled for you. The client's customers redeem directly through the travel support team.

Destinations the client's customers can choose

End customers choose from curated resort stays, cruise getaways, and city escapes. The breadth of choice keeps the incentive appealing across a client's whole audience, which is what makes it a reliable conversion lever rather than a niche offer.

Adding an incentive to a client campaign, step by step

Say a client's paid campaign is driving traffic but stalling at the demo-booking step, and the client's instinct is to run a discount or pour more budget into media. The agency proposes a sharper lever. First, it pinpoints the exact drop-off: strong clicks, weak form completions. Then it designs an incentive offer, layering a travel reward onto the call to action so booking a demo carries more pull without cutting the client's price.

The agency launches it as a clean test: incentivized creative against a control audience, tracking conversion rate, cost per acquisition with the reward factored in, and downstream close rate. When the incentivized cohort converts meaningfully better at a defensible CPA, the agency has a measurable win to report and a repeatable play to extend across the client's other campaigns. The end customers redeem their rewards directly with the travel support team, so neither the agency nor the client carries any fulfillment overhead. The agency has added a conversion tool competitors are not bringing to the pitch.

Promotion calendar: incentive ideas by campaign season

Aligning incentive pushes with the client's calendar keeps the lever sharp.

  • Q1 (Jan-Mar): New-year demand. Use incentives to lift lead-gen and demo bookings while budgets reset.
  • Q2 (Apr-Jun): Spring buying and event seasons. Layer rewards onto high-intent campaigns and measure lift against control.
  • Q3 (Jul-Sep): Slower summer stretch. Deploy incentives to keep pipeline warm without discounting the client's product.
  • Q4 (Oct-Dec): Peak spend and holiday promotions. Use rewards to differentiate the client's offer in the noisiest quarter and to fuel referral flows into the new year.

How agencies prove incentive lift to clients

The advantage of an incentive over a discount or more media is that it is measurable. Run incentivized creative against a control audience and report conversion rate, cost per acquisition with the reward's fixed cost included, and downstream close rate or order value. Because the reward is a known cost per conversion rather than a variable price cut, the client sees a clean return, and the agency gets a defensible before-and-after to put in the next QBR. Over several campaigns, that data becomes a repeatable playbook you can extend to new clients and new funnels.

Getting an incentive program started for a client

Setup is a short call to identify the funnel stage that stalls and design the incentive offer around the action that needs a lift, whether that is a form fill, a demo booking, or a final purchase. The agency launches it as a clean test against a control group, and the client's end customers redeem directly with the travel support team, so neither the agency nor the client carries fulfillment overhead. Agencies typically go live within days and can white-label the incentive as part of their own conversion services.

Frequently asked questions

How is a customer incentive better than a discount for clients?

It raises perceived value at the point of decision without cutting price, so the client's offer converts better while protecting margin, and it gives your agency a differentiated tool to pitch.

Can we measure the lift?

Yes. Because the incentive attaches to a defined action, you can compare incentivized versus control audiences on conversion, CPA, and average order value.

Where does an incentive fit in a campaign?

Anywhere the funnel stalls, from lead-gen form fills and demo bookings to the final purchase and post-sale referral flows.

Does it help win new client business?

It does. Including an incentive strategy in proposals differentiates your agency to high-ticket clients who are tired of discount-driven results.

How fast can we deploy for a client?

Most programs are live within days, with certificates issued on demand so the incentive can launch with the campaign.

How do we price an incentive into a client's campaign budget?

Treat the reward as a fixed, known cost per conversion rather than a variable discount. That makes it easy to model against target CPA and to show the client a clean return.

Can we white-label the incentive as part of our services?

Yes. Agencies present the incentive strategy as their own conversion offering, with the travel support team handling redemption behind the scenes, so it strengthens your proposals without adding operational load.

Does an incentive replace the media budget?

No. It works alongside media as a conversion lever. Media buys the traffic; the incentive raises the rate at which that traffic converts, which often improves the return on the spend you are already making.

Which clients benefit most from a customer incentive?

Clients selling considered or higher-ticket products, where buyers hesitate and a discount would erode margin, see the strongest lift. The reward gives their prospects a reason to act without the client cutting price.

How do we present this to a skeptical client?

Lead with the measurement. Propose a small incentivized test against a control audience, agree the success metric up front, and let the conversion and CPA data make the case before scaling it across the account.
