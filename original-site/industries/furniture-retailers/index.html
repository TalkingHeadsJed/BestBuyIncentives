Incentives for furniture retailers

Furniture retailers drive traffic and protect average ticket by giving shoppers a reason to buy beyond a markdown. A customer-facing reward lifts showroom traffic within days and closes the sale on value, so you escape the discount cycle that trains buyers to wait for the next sale.

By Karl Kramer, CEO of Best Buy Incentives
Published August 26, 2026 · Updated September 20, 2026

Why discounting hurts furniture retailers specifically

Furniture is a category buyers have been trained to never pay full price for. Between holiday sales, financing events, and clearance blitzes, the customer assumes a markdown is always around the corner, so every discount you run reinforces the habit of waiting. Promo fatigue sets in fast, and each event has to be deeper than the last to move the same traffic. That is a margin spiral, not a strategy.

The problem compounds because furniture margins already absorb delivery, warehousing, and floor-sample wear. A price cut on a sofa or bedroom set is a large bite out of a number that has to cover all of that. Competing on price alone means the store with the biggest markdown and the loudest ad wins, and that store is rarely the most profitable one by year end.

Furniture store promotion ideas beyond discounts

The retailers who break the discount cycle give shoppers something a competitor cannot simply match with a lower sticker:

  • Getaway-with-purchase event. Every customer who buys above a set ticket earns a travel reward. It draws traffic like a sale without cutting the price on your floor.
  • Room-package upsell. Trigger the reward at a full-room threshold so shoppers build a larger order to qualify, lifting average ticket.
  • Slow-season traffic driver. Run the reward in your quiet months to pull traffic forward without a storewide markdown.
  • Design-consultation close. Offer the reward when a customer books and buys through your design service, rewarding your highest-value path.

Each idea gives the customer an exciting reason to buy today while your price, and your margin, stay intact.

Furniture sales closing techniques that protect margin

On the showroom floor, the discount usually gets offered because the salesperson does not know how to answer "we want to think about it." A margin-safe close starts with diagnosis, not price:

  • Confirm the pieces fit the room and the customer's taste before anything else.
  • Ask what would make today the right day to have it delivered.
  • Surface the real hesitation, whether it is fit, timing, or comparison shopping, and answer that specific concern.
  • Only then present the reward as the reason to place the order now rather than leave and lose momentum.

This sequence keeps the conversation on value. By the time the reward appears, the customer already wants the furniture, and the getaway simply removes the reason to wait.

How the reward works in a furniture sale, step by step

  1. Qualify. The associate confirms the pieces are right and the customer is ready to buy.
  2. Resolve hesitation. Delivery timing, fabric choice, or financing gets settled.
  3. Present the reward. Once the order is essentially agreed, the associate introduces the travel reward that comes with buying today.
  4. Close the order. The reward turns a "let us sleep on it" into a written order before the customer leaves.
  5. Redemption is handled for you. The customer books their trip through the travel support team, so your staff stays focused on the floor.

Destinations your customers can choose

Shoppers pick from curated resort stays, cruise getaways, and city escapes, so the reward appeals to newlyweds furnishing a first home and empty-nesters redoing a great room alike. The breadth of choice is part of why a getaway outpulls a markdown: everyone sees a trip they would actually take.

A furniture sale with a travel voucher, step by step

A shopper has been eyeing a sectional and a dining set for a new home but says the classic line: "We love it, but let us think about it." Most floors answer that with a coupon. Here, the associate first confirms the pieces fit: "Are these the right pieces for the space, or is something still not quite right?" The shopper confirms they are perfect; the hesitation is just the size of the total.

The associate holds price and reframes: "I understand. These are pieces you'll live with for years. If you place the order today, I can include a getaway certificate as a thank-you, which is something a coupon at the next store won't give you." The shopper now weighs a full room of furniture plus a trip against walking away and starting over. They write the order before leaving, the store keeps its margin intact, and the customer redeems the getaway directly with the travel team. The associate simply issued the certificate on the spot and moved on to the next guest.

Promotion calendar: furniture incentive ideas by season

Furniture buying follows predictable rhythms you can build rewards around.

  • Winter (Jan-Feb): Run a getaway-with-purchase event during the post-holiday lull to pull traffic forward without a clearance markdown.
  • Spring (Mar-May): Target movers and remodelers, and set the qualifying threshold at a full-room package to lift average order value.
  • Summer (Jun-Aug): Anchor the reward to the July 4th and Labor Day windows so you draw sale-season traffic while holding floor prices.
  • Fall (Sep-Dec): Use the reward for pre-holiday delivery deadlines and design-consultation bookings, rewarding your highest-value path into the new year.

Because the reward is not a markdown, running it across seasons never trains shoppers to wait for a deeper discount.

How furniture retailers measure the payoff

Measure the campaign against your usual sale event, not against a blank month. Track showroom traffic, close rate, and average order value during the window, since the qualifying threshold is designed to lift ticket rather than lower it. Compare gross margin held versus the margin you would have given away in a comparable markdown event, and watch delivery-scheduled orders as a sign that shoppers committed rather than deferred. Over several campaigns, the telling metric is whether traffic holds without ever discounting the floor, which confirms you have broken the promo-fatigue cycle instead of feeding it.

Getting a furniture program started

A single setup call sets your qualifying threshold, the campaign length, and the reward mix, and certificates issue on demand so associates never wait on paperwork. Coach the floor on timing: confirm the pieces fit, resolve the real hesitation, then present the getaway as the reason to write the order today. Redemption runs entirely through the travel support team, so a busy showroom adds no fulfillment work. Independents and multi-store chains launch the same way, usually within a few days, and can run the first campaign around the next holiday window.

Frequently asked questions

Will a reward really drive more traffic than a sale?

It draws traffic like a sale while protecting price. Shoppers respond to a memorable getaway with purchase, and because you are not discounting the floor, the traffic arrives without eroding margin.

How does this lift average ticket?

Set the qualifying threshold at a full-room or higher price point and shoppers build a larger order to earn the reward, which raises average order value instead of lowering it.

Does it break the promo-fatigue cycle?

Yes. A reward is not a markdown, so it does not train customers to wait for a deeper discount next month. You give a reason to buy now that resets nothing about your pricing.

How many certificates do furniture retailers typically run?

Hundreds per quarter is common for multi-store retailers, issued on demand so associates always have the tool during a campaign.

How quickly can we launch a campaign?

Most retailers are live within days. A short setup call defines the threshold, campaign window, and program mix.

Will a getaway reward work for a single-store independent retailer?

Yes. Certificates are issued on demand with no minimum showroom size, so a single independent can run the same value-add campaign a multi-store chain uses.

How do we present the reward without cheapening a premium collection?

Introduce it after the customer has chosen the pieces, framed as a thank-you for the purchase rather than a discount on it. Timing keeps the collection premium and the reward a bonus.
