TITLE: HOW TO USE A DISCOUNTED TRAVEL VOUCHER IN A HIGH-TICKET CLOSE

AUDIENCE: Sales managers and representatives
TARGET LENGTH: 10-12 minutes

OPENING

Today we are adding one tool to the closing process: a discounted travel voucher.

This is not a replacement for discovery, product fit, value, or a direct closing question. It is an added-value offer for an eligible customer after the core purchase already makes sense.

The objective is simple: give a qualified buyer another reason to act without immediately reducing the base price.

PART 1: EARN THE RIGHT TO PRESENT THE OFFER

Before mentioning the voucher, confirm five things.

First, the customer has a real need.

Second, the product or service fits that need.

Third, the customer understands the core value.

Fourth, the purchase is workable and the necessary decision-makers are involved.

Fifth, timing or desire for additional value is the genuine remaining issue.

Ask yourself: Would this purchase make sense for the customer without the promotion?

If the answer is no, do not present the voucher yet. Keep working on fit and value, offer a more appropriate option, or stop.

PART 2: DIAGNOSE THE OBJECTION

When the customer hesitates, do not assume price is the problem.

Ask: What specifically would you like to consider - the fit, the investment, the timing, another option, or involving someone else?

Then confirm the answer.

Ask: If we resolve that concern, would you be comfortable moving forward, or is something else still unresolved?

A discounted travel voucher can help with timing or a desire for added value. It cannot repair poor fit, unaffordability, missing authority, weak proof, mistrust, or implementation risk.

PART 3: CONFIRM ELIGIBILITY

Before presenting the offer, confirm that the purchase meets the campaign rules.

Know the qualifying purchase, campaign dates, combination rules, voucher terms, recipient costs, restrictions, deadlines, and support process.

Do not invent an exception during the close. If you are uncertain, ask the manager before making the offer.

PART 4: PRESENT IT ACCURATELY

Use this language:

For qualifying purchases during this campaign, we include a discounted travel voucher. May I show you exactly what it provides, what the recipient pays, and the applicable dates?

Then explain the complete offer.

Tell the customer what travel value is discounted and what is included.

Tell the customer what the recipient pays or remains responsible for.

Explain eligibility, reservation or redemption steps, restrictions, deadlines, and support.

Give the customer access to the complete terms before asking for the decision.

Do not describe the voucher as cash. Do not hide recipient costs. Do not imply that it repairs a concern with the product itself.

PART 5: ASK WHETHER IT CHANGES THE DECISION

After the customer understands the offer, ask:

Does that added value affect your timing?

If the answer is yes, ask directly:

Would you like to move forward?

Then stop talking and allow the customer to answer.

If the answer is no, return to diagnosis. Do not add pressure or immediately offer another concession.

PART 6: HANDLE COMMON RESPONSES

CUSTOMER: YOUR PRICE IS TOO HIGH.

REPRESENTATIVE: Is the investment outside the available budget, or are you uncertain the value justifies it?

If the issue is value, reconnect the offer to the customer's stated priorities and provide relevant proof. If the purchase is not affordable, explore a suitable option or stop. Do not use the voucher to create affordability.

CUSTOMER: I NEED TO THINK ABOUT IT.

REPRESENTATIVE: Of course. What would you most like to think through so I can provide the right information?

Identify the concern before deciding whether the voucher is relevant.

CUSTOMER: I WANT TO SHOP AROUND.

REPRESENTATIVE: That makes sense. Which parts of the decision do you most want to compare?

Help the customer compare price, scope, service, implementation, warranty, support, operating cost, and risk. The voucher can be included in that total-value comparison, but it should not prevent reasonable research.

CUSTOMER: CAN YOU GIVE ME A DISCOUNT INSTEAD?

REPRESENTATIVE: If the price changed, would you be ready to move forward, or is another concern still unresolved?

If a concession is appropriate, follow manager approval rules and exchange it for a defined change in scope, quantity, terms, timing, or commitment.

PART 7: RECORD THE OUTCOME

Every eligible opportunity should record whether the voucher was presented, whether it was issued, the objection category, any discount, the decision, average order value, gross profit, cancellation, and revenue.

Managers will use that information to compare qualified close rate, cycle time, discount frequency, gross profit, cancellations, attributable revenue, and commission.

We are not measuring whether representatives like the offer. We are measuring whether it creates profitable sales and a sound customer experience.

CLOSING

Remember the sequence.

Confirm the purchase makes sense.

Diagnose the real obstacle.

Confirm eligibility.

Call it a discounted travel voucher.

Explain the complete recipient terms.

Ask whether the added value affects timing.

Ask directly for the decision.

Record the outcome.

Used at the right moment, this gives the customer another reason to say yes and gives the sales team an alternative to surrendering margin.
