When a Purchase Incentive Can Resolve an Objection

Learn when a purchase incentive can resolve timing or value-added objections—and when poor fit, affordability, authority, or trust require another response.

Schedule a Campaign Consultation

A purchase incentive is a powerful way to add value, influence timing, and protect the base price. When it follows a strong value conversation, it gives customers an exciting final reason to choose now.

When an incentive creates a stronger close

An incentive is most relevant when:

  • The product clearly fits the customer's need.
  • The customer understands the core value.
  • The investment is affordable.
  • The required decision-makers are involved.
  • Important trust and implementation questions are resolved.
  • Timing or desire for additional value is the genuine remaining issue.
  • The customer qualifies under documented campaign rules.

In that situation, a discounted travel voucher provides an additional reason to complete the purchase during a real offer period.

Match the incentive to the decision

An incentive is most powerful when the customer sees the fit and value and wants an added reason to move now. Use discovery, proof, a tailored solution, and decision access to build the foundation; then let the incentive add momentum.

Use the diagnostic sequence

Ask:

  1. “Does the core purchase make sense without considering a promotion?”
  2. “What specifically remains unresolved?”
  3. “If that issue were resolved, would you be ready to move forward?”
  4. “Would additional value affect the timing of your decision?”

Introduce the voucher when the answers show that added value can help bring the decision forward.

Present the offer accurately

Call it a discounted travel voucher. Highlight the travel value, confirm eligibility, and give the customer a clear overview of reservation or redemption with the current program guide.

Position the voucher as the differentiated travel benefit it is: a memorable added value that complements a strong purchase decision.

Measure whether it works

Record eligibility, whether the voucher was presented, the objection category, discount amount, close outcome, average order value, gross profit, cancellation, issuance, and attributable revenue. Compare eligible deals with a credible baseline or controlled group.
