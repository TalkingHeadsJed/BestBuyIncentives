What the Product Is

A discounted travel voucher gives the recipient access to a specified travel benefit under the terms of that particular voucher.

Products may differ in destination, accommodation, cruise, ticket, duration, occupancy, activation, booking, recipient costs, availability, and restrictions.

The exact voucher—not a generic industry description—determines the experience.

Why High-Ticket Sellers Use Them

High-ticket prospects often compare options or request a discount after deciding that the product fits. A discounted travel voucher can:

  • Add perceived value without reducing the listed price.
  • Differentiate a business in a crowded market.
  • Give the salesperson an approved offer for qualifying buyers.
  • Support promotions, appointments, referrals, upgrades, and retention.
  • Create an experience the customer may remember beyond the transaction.

The voucher should not be used to conceal poor fit, affordability, missing evidence, or incomplete terms.

What the Customer Must Understand

Before relying on the offer, the customer should be able to answer:

  • What does this voucher provide?
  • What will I be responsible for paying?
  • How and when do I activate it?
  • How does booking work?
  • Which dates, destinations, inventory, or other restrictions apply?
  • When does it expire?
  • Who helps me if I have a question?

Best Buy Incentives' approved sales materials and implementation process should make these answers accessible to management, the salesperson, and the recipient.

Common Sales Applications

Closing qualified prospects

Present the voucher after the core purchase fits and timing remains the obstacle.

Protecting price

Compare the actual voucher and campaign cost with the gross-profit effect of a proposed discount.

Moving selected inventory

Use real eligibility and end dates for a defined product group.

Generating appointments and referrals

Tie the offer to a qualifying behavior and measure lead quality through the completed sale.

Retention and customer appreciation

Reward a milestone or relationship without confusing the program with an employee reward.

A Managed Program, Not Just a Voucher

The sales result depends on implementation:

  1. Choose the objective and audience.
  2. Select the appropriate voucher.
  3. Define eligibility and campaign rules.
  4. Prepare accurate customer-facing materials.
  5. Train the team.
  6. Track use and outcomes.
  7. Review customer questions and fulfillment experience.
