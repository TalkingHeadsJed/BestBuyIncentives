When the Prospect Likes the Offer but Still Will Not Decide

Your salesperson did the work. The customer has a genuine need. The solution fits. Then the conversation stalls:

  • “I need to think about it.”
  • “I want to shop around.”
  • “Can you do anything on the price?”
  • “Maybe next month.”

Reducing price may save the transaction, but it also removes margin and can teach buyers to wait for a concession. More pressure risks the relationship.

What if your team could add something memorable instead?

Best Buy Incentives helps businesses use discounted travel vouchers as customer purchase incentives—giving qualified buyers additional value while allowing the seller to protect the value of the core product.

A Closing Tool, Not a Substitute for Selling

The discounted travel voucher works after the fundamentals are in place:

  1. The salesperson understands the customer's need.
  2. The recommended product or service fits.
  3. The customer understands the value.
  4. The actual remaining issue is timing or comparison.
  5. The salesperson presents the voucher accurately and asks for the decision.

The voucher does not replace discovery, proof, affordability, or trust. It gives a qualified customer another reason to act.

How the Program Works

1. Define the sales objective

We begin with the result: improve close rate, reduce unnecessary discounting, move selected inventory, support appointments, encourage referrals, or increase retention.

2. Select the appropriate discounted travel voucher

The offer must fit the audience, transaction, economics, and campaign. Each voucher has specific inclusions, recipient costs, deadlines, availability, and restrictions.

3. Prepare the team

Your representatives need to know who qualifies, when to present the offer, how to explain it, and what to record.

4. Launch and measure

Track offer use, close rate, discounts, gross profit, time to decision, customer response, and sales outcomes.
