Start With the Client's Revenue Problem

Determine whether the client needs qualified appointments, stronger close rate, price protection, higher order value, aged inventory movement, or referrals. Select the offer only after defining the objective, transaction economics, and target audience.

Suitable Client Profiles

The strongest candidates often have meaningful transaction margin, qualified buyers who stall on timing or request discounts, manageable sales volume, manager control, operational capacity, and reliable CRM outcomes.

Potential verticals include automotive, furniture, jewelry, home improvement, roofing, HVAC, boat/RV, wedding/event, and other high-ticket sales environments. Each client requires its own fit assessment.

Protect the Client With Accurate Communication

Agency creative and client sales language must use the same offer name, eligibility, dates, included value, recipient costs, restrictions, booking or redemption, and support information.

Never imply the discounted travel voucher covers a complete trip when recipient-paid components apply.

Connect Marketing With Sales Adoption

The client team must know when to present the offer, when not to use it, how to diagnose objections, how to explain terms, how managers approve exceptions, and what to record in CRM.

Campaign response will not become revenue if representatives ignore or misrepresent the offer.

Attribution Requirements

Track campaign, source, landing page, content, lead, consultation, qualified opportunity, proposal, actual voucher exposure, issuance, discount, sale, gross profit, closed revenue, and commission.

Use a credible baseline or control and distinguish total client revenue from incremental contribution.

Partnership Workflow

  1. Select and qualify the client opportunity.
  2. Define objective, eligible transaction, economics, and program.
  3. Approve terms, creative, sales language, and fulfillment.
  4. Implement CRM events and revenue reporting.
  5. Train the client sales and management team.
  6. Launch a controlled campaign.
  7. Optimize or expand based on qualified revenue evidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the agency white-label the program?

Branding and commercial arrangements depend on the selected partnership and program. Confirm available options during the partner consultation.

Who supports the recipient?

The selected voucher program should define provider support, while the agency and client have documented escalation and communication responsibilities.

How does the agency make money?

Possible models depend on the partnership: strategy and campaign fees, implementation, ongoing optimization, media, reporting, or an approved commercial arrangement. Define compensation and attribution before client launch.
