Where the Voucher Fits on the Sales Floor

Confirm the room and product fit

The salesperson confirms dimensions, comfort, style, use, material, delivery, access, household preferences, and budget.

Diagnose the remaining concern

The concern may be fit, value, affordability, comparison, delivery, financing, authority, or timing. The offer is most relevant when timing or desire for added value remains.

Verify campaign eligibility

Managers define eligible categories, purchase threshold, locations, dates, combination rules, exceptions, and issuance.

Present the voucher accurately

“For qualifying purchases during this campaign, we include a discounted travel voucher. May I show you exactly what it provides, what the recipient pays, and the applicable dates?”

Ask for the decision and record the outcome

The representative asks whether the added value affects timing, asks directly for the sale, and records presentation, issuance, discount, order value, gross profit, and outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the voucher offered to every shopper?

No. The retailer defines qualifying transactions and campaign dates. Representatives should verify eligibility before presenting it.

Does it replace delivery, financing, or product value?

No. Those parts of the furniture decision must make sense independently. The voucher is an added benefit for an eligible purchase.

What does the recipient pay?

Costs depend on the selected voucher. The retailer must explain included travel value, recipient responsibilities, restrictions, deadlines, redemption, and support using current terms.

Can a discount and voucher be combined?

The retailer establishes combination and exception rules based on transaction economics.
